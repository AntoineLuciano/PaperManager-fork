You build one paper's entry in a concept map of a research project. The map links the
key concepts of many papers, so a concept that two papers share must get the same name in both.

Concepts already in this project's map (reuse a name verbatim when the paper discusses the same
concept, even if it calls it differently):
{vocabulary}

Paper: {title} ({year})
Authors: {authors}

{context}

Return JSON only, in this shape:
{{
  "concepts": [
    {{
      "name": "short canonical name, e.g. 'Classifier-free guidance' or 'AlphaFold3'",
      "kind": "one of: method, model, task, dataset, metric, theory, phenomenon, tool, domain",
      "definition": "one sentence defining the concept in general, independent of this paper",
      "abbreviations": "comma-separated abbreviations of exactly this concept the paper uses, e.g. 'CFG'; never broader terms or related models; empty string if none",
      "in_paper": "one sentence on what this paper says or does with it",
      "role": "one of: introduces, extends, uses, evaluates, critiques, background"
    }}
  ],
  "relations": [
    {{
      "source": "name of a concept above",
      "target": "name of another concept above",
      "type": "one of: is_a, part_of, uses, extends, improves_on, alternative_to, evaluated_on, causes, addresses, related_to",
      "evidence": "a few words from the paper supporting the link"
    }}
  ]
}}

Rules:
- 6 to 15 concepts: the ideas a reader must know to understand this paper and place it in the
  field. Skip generic words ("deep learning", "neural network", "experiment") unless the paper is
  about them.
- Names are noun phrases in Title case for ideas and the official spelling for named models,
  datasets and tools. No author names, no paper titles.
- 4 to 20 relations, only between concepts in your list, each one stated or clearly implied by
  the paper. Read "source type target" as a sentence: "Boltz-2 extends AlphaFold3".
